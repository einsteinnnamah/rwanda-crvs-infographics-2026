A death certificate is demanded when land changes hands. Land mostly changes hands when a man dies.

Certificates unlock succession, land transfer and bank accounts, which sit mostly with men. 22,310 women and 16,211 men went unregistered in 2025: a 13.9-point completeness gap.

A facility death is logged at the bedside. A home death depends on a family travelling to the cell office with two witnesses within 30 days. In rural districts, two in three deaths happen at home.

Death registration rose from 31.4% in 2019 to 50.5% in 2025, with a dip to 26.2% in 2021 and the steepest climb, 10.7 points, between 2022 and 2023. Births have stayed above 84% throughout, carried by facility deliveries.

Facilities document infections (40.0% against 26.9%). Community deaths, certified by verbal autopsy, lean to long illnesses (58.8%). A register that grows only through facilities will drift towards what hospitals see.

Three drivers, one gap · Rwanda's 2030 target is 80% completeness

Now close the gap.

Place, sex and time pull on the same 38,521 people. A woman who dies at home, with no land in her name, is the person the system is least likely to ever count. On its current trend the register reaches roughly 67% by 2030. Three levers, each aimed at one driver, decide whether it crosses 80%.

+8 ptsVillage health worker reporting

Let community health workers file a death notice by phone, so a home death enters the system without a journey. Targets place.

+7 ptsRemove barriers for women

Decouple certificates from inheritance, waive late fees for deceased mothers and daughters, link registration to social protection. Targets sex.

+5 ptsDigital registration points

Cell-level kiosks and mobile registration days that make the 30-day window reachable. Targets time.
