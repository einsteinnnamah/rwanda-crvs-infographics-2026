Male death registration reached 57.6%. Female registration reached 43.7%. That is 22,310 women unrecorded.

In Muhanga and Nyaruguru, two in three deaths occur in the community, away from any facility that could log them.

 Ch 05 →

17,712 of the 57,067 deaths registered in 2025 happened in earlier years. Mop-up campaigns are working through a backlog.

 Ch 01 →

Death registration rose from 31.4% to 50.5% in six years, with the steepest climb between 2022 and 2023.

The two lines

Births are solved. Deaths are not.

Birth registration has stayed above 84% for the whole period because almost every delivery happens in a facility that files the record. Death registration started at a third and only reached half in 2025. The space between the lines is a structural gap, not a data glitch.

The missing women

Everyone can show that half of deaths go unregistered. The sharper question is whose deaths. The answer is lopsided.

A death certificate is demanded when land changes hands. Land in Rwanda mostly changes hands when a man dies.Our reading of the data, not a quotation

Death certificates unlock succession, land title transfer and the closing of bank accounts. Those assets sit disproportionately with men, so a household has an immediate reason to register a man's death. When an elderly mother dies at home, the trip to the cell office with two witnesses inside 30 days has no equivalent pull.

The backlog and the clock

Two things happened in 2025 that a single completeness number hides. The register caught up on old deaths, and it started losing the race against the 30-day deadline.

Every death registered in 2025, by year it occurred
Occurred in 2025 · 39,355Occurred earlier · 17,712

Of 57,067 deaths registered during 2025, 31% had happened in earlier years: 3,195 from 2024 and 14,517 from 2023 or before. That is the footprint of nationwide mop-up campaigns, and it is why the completeness rate can rise faster than the system's day-to-day capacity.

Within the oldest group, women outnumber men: 4,414 women against 3,406 men aged 80 and over. Yet women's deaths are the ones least likely to be registered overall. The record under-counts exactly the group it should see most of.

Chapter 07Source · NISR, Figures 23 to 25

What the record says they died of

Where a death happens changes what gets written down. Hospitals document infections. Homes report long illnesses.

Community deaths are certified through verbal autopsy, usually after the fact and from a relative's account. That favours long, visible conditions and under-reports infections. If the registry only ever grows through facilities, the national cause-of-death profile will drift towards what hospitals see.

Chapter 09Rwanda's 2030 target · 80% completeness

Now close the gap.

On its current trend the register would reach roughly 67% by 2030. Three levers, each aimed at one of the drivers above, decide whether it crosses 80%. Move them.

Community health worker reporting+8%

Equip Rwanda's village health workers to file a death notice by phone, so a home death enters the system without a journey. Targets the place driver.

Remove barriers to registering women+7%

Decouple certificates from inheritance disputes, waive late fees for deceased mothers and daughters, and link registration to social protection records. Targets the sex driver.

Digital registration points+5%

Cell-level kiosks and mobile registration days that make the 30-day window reachable. Targets the time driver.
