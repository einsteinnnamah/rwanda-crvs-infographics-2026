SECTION 01

Place of Death: The Community Bottleneck

When someone dies in a hospital, staff log the death. When someone dies at home, grieving families must travel to the cell office with two witnesses within 30 days. That structural friction explains why rural districts lag.

57.9% Missing Women (22,310 deaths)

Why Does This Gap Exist?

In Rwanda, official death certificates are most often sought when property, land titles, or bank accounts need formal inheritance clearance. Because men more frequently hold legal title to household assets, families face strong economic incentives to register male deaths. Without inheritance triggers, female deaths are far more likely to go undeclared.

SECTION 03

2019–2025: Strong Gains, Timeliness Reversal, and the Backlog

Death registration rose from 31.4% in 2019 to 50.5% in 2025 (+19.1 percentage points). But 2025 brought two critical shifts: late in-year registrations tripled, and a massive historical backlog entered the system.

SECTION 05

Cause of Death: Facility vs. Community Divergence

Where a person dies fundamentally alters the documented cause of death. Hospital deaths are certified by doctors (MCCD), while community deaths rely on verbal autopsy (VA).

Health Facility vs. Community Cause Profiles

Distribution across 3 broad disease categories (Figure 25)

Key Insights on Cause Documentation

Noncommunicable Diseases (NCDs) Dominate Community Deaths

NCDs account for 58.8% of usable community deaths versus 49.5% in health facilities. Cardiovascular conditions, diabetes, and cancers frequently culminate at home.

Communicable Conditions Higher in Facilities (40.0% vs. 26.9%)

Severe infections and perinatal complications are actively brought to hospitals before death, making up 40.0% of facility fatalities compared to 26.9% in the community.

Injuries Claim Higher Share at Home (14.3% vs. 10.5%)

Fatal accidents, falls, and occupational injuries occurring away from clinics often result in on-scene or at-home expiration before hospital admission is possible.

Data Note on Usability:

In 2025, verbal autopsies scaled significantly: 18,922 community deaths had assigned causes (85.9% of registered community deaths), providing health planners with vital epidemiological visibility.
